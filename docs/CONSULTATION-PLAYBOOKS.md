# Industry consultation playbooks

The private Command Center at `/dashboard/consultations` is an industry-first discovery workspace. Its purpose is to turn one real operating workflow into a defensible next step—not to jump from a broad complaint to a software proposal.

## Yorkstead offer ladder

Every playbook uses the current commercial model:

| Step | Use it when | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Workflow Rescue** | The current flow, baseline, or highest-impact breakdown is still disputed. | A current-state map, ranked bottlenecks, and an Operations Systems Blueprint for one workflow. |
| **Workflow Sprint** | One bounded handoff has a clear trigger, finish line, owner, evidence set, and acceptance path. | A focused, usable implementation of that workflow. |
| **Department System** | Several records and handoffs inside one function must work together. | A connected department operating system with staged rollout. |
| **Company Operations System** | Multiple proven modules need a shared operating record and phased governance. | A modular company system delivered through agreed milestones. |

Yorkstead sells the agreed software as a purchased asset. The proposal defines delivered code, documentation, usage rights, third-party dependencies, milestones, and acceptance. Hosting and provider costs are separate. Continued Yorkstead support is optional.

## Before the conversation

1. Pick the industry closest to the workflow. Choose based on how work moves, not only the company’s NAICS label.
2. Read the **best fit**, **workflow patterns**, and **caution signals**.
3. Request a small evidence set representing a real case. Prefer the last completed job, order, load, shift, or case over a polished SOP.
4. Avoid receiving secrets or unnecessary sensitive records. Redacted examples, screenshots, field lists, and representative exports are usually enough for discovery.
5. Decide which baseline measures can be established honestly. A clearly labeled qualitative baseline is better than invented precision.

## During the conversation

Work through the six sections as a guide, not a script:

1. **Operating context** — why now, who owns the decision, and who does the work.
2. **Industry workflow** — trace a single representative case from trigger to finish.
3. **Friction and control** — find exceptions, judgment, evidence, and recovery paths.
4. **Outcome and baseline** — state the current condition, desired result, and smallest useful release.
5. **Commercial path** — recommend the smallest Yorkstead step supported by evidence.
6. **Implementation handoff** — identify data, access, review, acceptance, rollout, and operating ownership.

Ask for the last real example whenever the discussion becomes hypothetical. Capture disagreement explicitly; disagreement about the current state is often evidence that Workflow Rescue is the correct next step.

## Choosing the next offer

Recommend **Workflow Rescue** when any of these are true:

- the trigger, finish line, or authoritative record is unclear;
- office, field, floor, or management describe different workflows;
- no credible baseline exists;
- the requested build spans several unproven modules;
- integrations, permissions, or exception ownership are assumed;
- the business wants an implementation price before the operating boundary is understood.

Recommend a **Workflow Sprint** only when one path is already bounded, representative evidence exists, normal and failure cases can be tested, and a client owner can review and accept the result.

Move toward a **Department System** after multiple connected handoffs inside one function are understood. A **Company Operations System** should normally be phased from proven modules rather than proposed as an all-at-once replacement.

## Evidence and measurement rules

- Record the source and date behind important numbers.
- Separate observed data from estimates and anecdotes.
- Use ranges when source data is incomplete.
- Do not promise labor savings, revenue, throughput, search rankings, or ROI without a defensible baseline and an agreed measurement method.
- Treat safety, compliance, tax, payments, professional judgment, and customer commitments as explicit human-control boundaries unless responsibility is verified.

## Saving and handoff

Required prompts are marked with an asterisk, but incomplete discovery can still be saved. Stages remain **Draft**, **Discovery**, **Scoped**, and **Archived**.

**Copy briefing** produces Markdown containing:

- client and industry context;
- the related public service;
- the industry call objective and entry logic;
- relevant workflow patterns;
- all captured answers;
- baseline measures still needing validation; and
- the development-gate checklist.

The copied briefing is an Operations Systems Blueprint starting point. It is not an automatically approved proposal, estimate, architecture, ROI claim, or technical specification.

## Maintaining the library

- Keep industry IDs and field IDs stable after records exist.
- Add prompts only when the answer changes qualification, scope, architecture, acceptance, rollout, or ownership.
- Preserve at least one default industry for every public service so older service-based records remain readable.
- Keep each industry’s evidence, baseline measures, exceptions, and development gate specific enough to change a decision.
- Update the playbook and this guide together when Yorkstead’s offer ladder, ownership terms, or public services change.
